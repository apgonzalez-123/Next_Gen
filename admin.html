How a line gets here

1 · The database

Everything the engine may buy is built by tools/build_products.py straight from the desk’s own files — the securities export, the non-USD ideas, the FX sheet, the structured-note picks and the blended equity list — into data/products.json. Nothing is hand-entered here. Each instrument carries a fit vector: for every question the room is asked, how well that instrument answers it. Those come from real attributes, not labels: a bond’s composite rating and payment rank, a note’s barrier, coupon and tenor, a fund’s beta and realised volatility.

2 · The sleeve sizes

Risk appetite and horizon set what share of the risk budget is equity; an income mandate pulls out of it. Structured notes start at 8% and grow with the room’s appetite for leverage. A dollar view away from neutral funds an FX sleeve. Options are then carved out of equities — never added on top, because a covered call needs the stock behind it — sized by the leverage and income answers.

3 · The score

Every instrument on a sleeve’s shelf is scored against every question the room answered, weighted per sleeve. A continuous answer is scored against each guest individually and then averaged, not against the room’s average — a room split between 0% and 100% dollars is a divided room, not a 50% room. The axis scores combine as a blend of an arithmetic and a geometric mean, so a line has to be at least passable everywhere rather than excellent on most and wrong on one.

4 · The picks

Lines are taken one at a time on marginal fit, each discounted by how much it duplicates what the sleeve already holds, so it cannot buy the same exposure twice through two tickers. No more than 60% of a sleeve’s lines may share a sector. How many lines depends on how much the sleeve is asked to carry and how divided the room was, so the asset classes do not all hold the same number.
